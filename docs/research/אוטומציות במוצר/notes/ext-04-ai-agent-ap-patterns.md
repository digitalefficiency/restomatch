## 1. Executive summary: seven patterns that matter for RestoMatch

1. **Every serious finance agent ships in "recommend" mode and makes autonomy opt-in, scoped and reversible.** Ramp Policy Agent only recommends until an admin enables auto-approval by amount, merchant category or department. Microsoft's Payables Agent "never posts invoices without explicit human approval". Tipalti: "Your team reviews and approves every bill." Brex auto-approves only "low-risk" items. No reputable vendor lets an agent move money unsupervised by default.
2. **"Touchless" is a dial, not a fact.** Vic.ai defines Autopilot as invoices whose fields are predicted at ≥95% confidence, and customers set the threshold. Lowering the threshold raises the touchless rate and the error rate together. Published rates cluster at 70–85% after about 6 months of learning on PO-backed invoices, all self-reported.
3. **Agents learn from corrections per vendor, and the first month is the weakest.** Ramp says 85% of fields are right first time. Pepper says over 85% first-pass accuracy "after the first four weeks". Vic.ai quotes its 85% figure "by month 6". The product has to survive weeks 1–4 when the agent is worst.
4. **Supplier communication is the newest frontier, and it is mostly draft-first.** Microsoft's Supplier Communications Agent can send PO follow-ups automatically **or** save them as drafts for review. Medius Supplier Conversations *answers* supplier emails but "does not perform any actions". Spendflo describes a debit-memo agent that tracks a short-delivery claim until a matching credit memo arrives. Nobody publishes a recovered-money metric. This is the earlier note's gap, confirmed again.
5. **The documented failures are about trust and liability, not arithmetic.** Air Canada was held liable for its chatbot's misinformation (Feb 2024). Klarna rehired humans after its CEO said cost had been "a too predominant evaluation factor" (May 2025). Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end-2027. AP-specific risks are prompt injection through invoice content changing bank details, and agents that **over-hold** good invoices for trivial formatting mismatches.
6. **Conversational assistants over financial data are converging on "the LLM calls a governed metric layer, never free-writes SQL".** The M1 Finance and dbt case, a 2026 arXiv benchmark, and practitioner guides all say this. RestoMatch's Ask already follows the pattern: read-only tools plus a grounding check on every ₪ figure. That is a strength to keep.
7. **In foodservice B2B, the AI sits on the distributor side.** Choco, Pepper and Ordermentum turn restaurant orders sent by WhatsApp, voicemail, text or photo into ERP orders for the **supplier**. None of them audits the supplier's invoice for the **restaurant**. That leaves room for RestoMatch to be the restaurant-side agent that checks what the distributor's agent produced.

## 2. Player-by-player: what is automated, what humans confirm, claimed outcomes, price, and the lesson

### 2.1 Ramp: Policy Agent and AP agent (US, SMB to mid-market)

- **Automates.** Policy Agent: expense submitted → agent reads the receipt and memo against the written expense policy → recommends approve or reject, with a reason. In the GA release (13 Jan 2026) it acts as "first-pass reviewer", and controllers choose which clearly in-policy expenses it may approve automatically ([Ramp Policy Agent GA](https://ramp.com/blog/ramp-policy-agent-ga-launch)). AP agent: invoice arrives → line-item coding from the vendor's history and invoice context → approver recommendation plus an AI summary for the approver. An April 2026 post says it learns from corrections and was rolled out to all Ramp Plus customers by 28 Apr 2026 ([Ramp AP agent, Apr 2026](https://ramp.com/blog/ap-agent-processes-invoices)).
- **Human confirms.** Auto-approval is **off by default**: "the agent only recommends actions and does not approve anything until a workflow enables it". Admins scope auto-approval by amount, spend program, department or merchant category. When policy is ambiguous the agent marks the expense "Requires review", and reviewers can override any decision ([Ramp support: Use Policy Agent for approvals](https://support.ramp.com/hc/en-us/articles/47618318137875-Use-Policy-Agent-for-approvals); [support.ramp.com/use-policy-agent-for-approvals](https://support.ramp.com/use-policy-agent-for-approvals)).
- **Claimed outcomes (vendor).** "99% accuracy", escalating only 10–15% of expenses to humans (July 2025 launch) ([Ramp agents announcement](https://ramp.com/blog/ramp-agents-announcement)). Help centre: early customers cut manual reviews by about 85% at 99%+ accuracy ([Ramp Policy agent overview](https://support.ramp.com/hc/en-us/articles/44072387128979-Policy-agent)). AP: 85% of coding right first time, and about 90% of approval recommendations accepted, per a webinar page ([Ramp webinar](https://ramp.com/webinars/agentmaxxing-for-intake-to-pay)). "Accuracy" is not defined anywhere I could find.
- **Price.** Policy Agent is Ramp Plus only ([Ramp support](https://support.ramp.com/hc/en-us/articles/44072387128979-Policy-agent)). Plus pricing per seat: UNVERIFIED.
- **Lesson. COPY** the configuration grammar: autonomy defaults to *recommend*. The owner turns on auto-approve with explicit scope (≤ ₪X, this supplier, this discrepancy type), and anything ambiguous falls back to "requires review". **COPY** the AI summary on the approval card. **LEAPFROG**: Ramp checks policy compliance. RestoMatch checks **contract and price compliance against the supplier's own previous prices**, a harder and more valuable target in ₪.

### 2.2 Brex: agent "mesh" (US)

- **Automates.** A Review Agent auto-approves low-risk expenses and requests, escalates exceptions, and follows up with employees who are out of compliance. An Audit Agent monitors expenses and sorts potential violations by risk level ([Brex AI agents](https://www.brex.com/platform/brex-ai)).
- **Human confirms.** Brex's own wording is "human-in-the-loop control for important decisions" with "complete audit trails" ([Brex](https://brex.com/intelligence)).
- **Claimed outcomes.** Up to 99% automation of expense workflows, reported by VentureBeat on 5 Jan 2026 and syndicated on Pulse ([Pulse/VentureBeat](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/brex-bets-on-less-orchestration-as-it-builds-an-agent-mesh-for-autonomous-finance-ac7fb3c9-99bd-4037-9078-54328886b15a/)). WebProNews attributes "99% of expense reports without human intervention" to a CFO post on X ([WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/brexs-ai-agent-handles-99-of-expense-reports-without-human-intervention-and-the-implications-are-staggering/)). That is secondhand and contradicts Brex's own stress on escalation. **Treat as UNVERIFIED.**
- **A useful engineering confession.** In a Plaid customer Q&A, Brex says it first graded memos on a 1–5 scale, but "differentiating between a two and a three is hard for both a human and an agent" ([Plaid × Brex](https://plaid.com/customer-stories/brex-effects-2026/)).
- **Lesson. COPY** the Brex finding: use **binary or three-state verdicts** (OK / needs review / block), not fine-grained scores, for anything a human or an agent must act on. RestoMatch's severity tiers should stay coarse. **AVOID** publishing "99%" headlines. They invite exactly the credibility gap seen here.

### 2.3 Vic.ai: confidence-gated Autopilot

- **Automates.** Invoice → header and line-item prediction (coding, approver) → invoices whose fields are all at ≥95% confidence go to "Autopilot" with no human review ([Vic.ai FAQ](https://www.vic.ai/faq); [Vic.ai autonomous invoice processing](https://www.vic.ai/products/autonomous-invoice-processing)). Confidence scores are shown at header and line level. **Customers set the thresholds.**
- **Claimed outcomes (vendor case studies).** Up to an 85% no-touch rate and a 70% Autopilot rate within 6 months ([Vic.ai product](https://www.vic.ai/products/autonomous-invoice-processing)). Customer figures: a large US distributor at 76% no-touch ([Vic.ai case](https://vic.ai/resources/large-us-distribution-company-processes-76-percent-of-invoices-without-any-human-touch)); an accounting firm at 84% no-touch and 76% Autopilot ([Vic.ai case](https://vic.ai/resources/case-study-accounting-and-tax-advisory-firm-leverages-vic-ai-to-process-invoices-faster-than-bill-com)).
- **Independent framing.** A reviewer calls the touchless rate "a dial you set": lower the threshold and touchless rises while error risk climbs ([procurement-ai-agents review](https://procurement-ai-agents.pages.dev/blog/vic-ai-invoice-automation-review)). This is a low-authority site, but the logic holds.
- **Lesson. COPY** the two-number disclosure: separate "no-touch" (nobody edited a field) from "Autopilot" (nobody even looked). **LEAPFROG** with a third number that matters to the owner: "₪ caught without anyone looking", meaning discrepancies flagged on invoices nobody had to open. RestoMatch's OCR review threshold is already 0.85 (`packages/ocr/src/types.ts:92`, per INT-03). Expose it as a disclosed dial with a measured error rate, not a hidden constant.

### 2.4 Tipalti: single-purpose agents

- **Automates.** One agent per task: Invoice Capture (header, line items, FX, VAT, tables), PO Matching (reads line descriptions "in context, so a bill finds its PO match even when the wording differs"; flags quantity, price or currency discrepancies), approver routing, W-9 extraction, and ERP-sync error resolution ([Tipalti AI](https://tipalti.com/accounts-payable-software/finance-ai); [Tipalti AP automation](https://tipalti.com/solutions/ap-automation/)).
- **Human confirms.** "Your team reviews and approves every bill" ([Tipalti invoice management](https://tipalti.com/ap-automation/invoice-management/)). The earlier Tipalti Pi product was framed as exposing areas needing attention "while ensuring that a human is in control of financial actions" ([The Paypers on Tipalti Pi](https://thepaypers.com/fraud-and-fincrime/news/tipalti-reveals-its-ai-and-ml-solution-tipalti-pi-for-accounts-payable)).
- **Claimed outcomes / price.** No agent-specific figures found. Price: UNVERIFIED (quote-based).
- **Lesson. COPY** semantic line matching ("עגבניות שרי 250 גר'" on the invoice ↔ "שרי אדום" in the catalog). This is the Hebrew-catalog problem. **COPY** the single-purpose agent decomposition, because each agent gets its own measurable accuracy number and its own kill switch.

### 2.5 Stampli "Billy": the anti-agent positioning

- **Automates.** Billy is an AI layer across procure-to-pay: coding, approver prediction and PO matching. A Nov 2025 release pitches it as "Not an Agent — an AI Employee" and attacks rivals' "siloed AI agents" ([finanzen.net / Stampli PR](https://www.finanzen.net/nachricht/aktien/stampli-s-ai-billy-marks-three-years-of-advanced-vendor-management-15223515)). Vendor communication goes through a Vendor Portal and threads on each invoice, not an autonomous messaging agent ([Stampli vendor management](https://stampli.com/?p=23058)).
- **Human confirms.** Billy's suggestions are subject to AP-team validation ([Billy the Bot](https://www.stampli.com/billy-the-bot-stamplis-ai-copilot/)). Commentary says Stampli has resisted shipping an agent that pays bills without supervision ([yespress](https://yespress.io/stampli)). That is commentary, not official.
- **Claimed outcomes.** Over $90B of annual spend for 1,600+ customers, and a record 2M invoices a month (Nov 2025 PR). Case studies: Superior Masonry reclaimed 40–60 hours/week; Renova cut processing time by 50% ([casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/stampli/case-study/superior-masonry-cements-ap-efficiency-with-stampli-and-sage-intacct-construction); [Renova](https://www.casestudies.com/company/stampli/case-study/renova-energy-reduces-invoice-processing-time-by-50)). All vendor-published.
- **Lesson. COPY** "communication lives on the invoice": every supplier message, photo and claim is threaded on the invoice or discrepancy record, so the audit trail falls out of how the product is built. **AVOID** "agent" branding with owner-operators. "It finds the money; you press send" is more credible to a skeptical restaurateur.

### 2.6 Medius Supplier Conversations: answer, don't act

- **Automates.** Supplier email ("when will invoice X be paid?") → an LLM (OpenAI-based, trained on Medius) answers 24/7 from AP data ([Medius Supplier Conversations](https://www.medius.com/solutions/supplier-conversations/)).
- **Human confirms.** The scope limit is deliberate: it "does not perform any actions in Medius on behalf of the supplier or the AP team".
- **Claimed outcome.** Inbox handling falls from about 8 hours/week to about 30 minutes, a 94% cut (vendor marketing, undated in results).
- **Lesson.** For RestoMatch the direction is reversed: **the restaurant chases the supplier**. The pattern still transfers. **COPY** "read-only conversational agent first, write actions later". **Leapfrog idea**: when a supplier replies to a claim (WhatsApp or email), the agent *classifies* the reply (accepted / credit note promised / disputed / ignored) and updates the claim state. It never replies on its own.

### 2.7 Microsoft Dynamics 365: Payables Agent and Supplier Communications Agent

- **Payables Agent (Business Central, public preview from v26.3, 2025).** It watches a mailbox for vendor invoices → AI reads them → creates **purchase-invoice drafts** → "agent supervisors" review, confirm or change suggested fields. The scope depends on configuration and the agent's confidence. **New vendors are blocked** until a person unblocks them. The FAQ says it "never posts invoices without explicit human approval", and vendor creation and draft→invoice conversion are classed as **high-risk actions requiring mandatory approval** ([MS Learn: Payables Agent](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-gb/Dynamics365/business-central/payables-agent); [MS Learn FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/faqs-payables-agent); [Demiliani, 10 Jul 2025](https://demiliani.com/2025/07/10/dynamics-365-business-central-introducing-the-payables-agent/)).
- **Supplier Communications Agent (D365 SCM).** Purchase-order follow-ups go to vendors either **sent automatically or saved as drafts that need human review**. Vendor replies are parsed and proposed changes (e.g. confirmed dates) are applied after review. Billing is in Copilot Studio credits ([MS Learn overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/procurement/supplier-com-agent-overview); [MS Learn FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/faq-supplier-communications-agent); [apply email changes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/procurement/supplier-com-agent-apply-email-changes)). The "send or draft" detail was seen only on a third-party mirror of the Learn page, so the exact wording is **UNVERIFIED** on learn.microsoft.com.
- **Lesson. COPY** the explicit **risk-class table**: list each action the system can take, tag it low or high risk, and make high-risk actions (new supplier, change of bank details, outbound supplier message, anything that changes a payable amount) permanently human-gated whatever the confidence. **COPY** "blocked new vendor". This is the cleanest published version of the "propose-then-confirm" UX, and it matches AGENTS.md prime directive 2 in spirit.

The one named customer quote is about 4 hours/week. Tiering per a secondary blog: Essentials $75/mo, Advanced $275/mo for the Finance agent ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/quickbooks-ai)), UNVERIFIED against Intuit.
- **Xero JAX (Xerocon, 2026).** Cash Flow Actions *suggest* delaying non-critical bills to protect payroll. The user reviews, adjusts and executes. "Bill protection" reviews bills **before payment** and flags unusual amounts, changed bank details or unfamiliar suppliers. Document chasing emails clients for missing receipts. ## 3. Cross-cutting design patterns

### 3.1 Approval-rule engines with confidence thresholds

From the evidence, the shared shape is:

1. **Two independent gates.** (a) *Extraction confidence*: is the data right? (Vic.ai ≥95%; RestoMatch OCR 0.85.) (b) *Policy/risk rule*: is it allowed? (amount, supplier, category; Ramp, Basware autonomy gates). Auto-action requires **both**.
2. **Default to "requires review" on ambiguity** (Ramp).
3. **Scoped auto-approve set by the owner**, not by the vendor (Ramp: amount, merchant, department).
4. **Permanent human gates for high-risk classes** regardless of confidence (Microsoft: new vendor, posting; Xero: changed bank details).
5. **Coarse verdicts** (Brex's 1–5 scale lesson).
6. **Learning loop from corrections, per vendor** (Ramp, Pepper, Vic.ai), with a 4-week to 6-month ramp.

RestoMatch's approvals engine already has per-tenant `approvalThresholds` and auto-approves `minor-info` findings on small runs (INT-03 §5). The missing pieces are (1) a disclosed, owner-visible **autonomy setting per discrepancy type and supplier** and (2) a **measured** auto-approve error rate. Both touch `approvals/engine.ts` **[IMM]**, so they go in a human-authored PR.

### 3.2 Propose-then-confirm UX

The cleanest published versions are Microsoft (drafts, supervisor confirms or changes, mandatory approval for high-risk) and Xero (suggested plan → review, adjust, execute in place). Common features: the draft is a **first-class editable object**, not a chat message. The change is shown as a diff against the current state. Confirm is one tap. Reject asks for a reason, and that reason trains the agent. For RestoMatch: a claim letter or supplier WhatsApp message is a draft object with the ₪ amount, the evidence lines, and "שלח" / "ערוך" / "לא עכשיו" buttons. AGENTS.md already requires a human for every send.

### 3.3 Agentic vendor communication

Maturity ladder seen in the market:
- **L0, answer only** (Medius Supplier Conversations: "does not perform any actions").
- **L1, draft for review** (Microsoft Supplier Comms draft mode; Spendflo debit memo drafting).
- **L2, send routine follow-ups automatically within a policy** (Microsoft auto-send for PO follow-ups; Intuit payment reminders; Xero document chasing).
- **L3, negotiate or settle**: not found shipped anywhere for money disputes.

For a ₪ claim against a supplier the owner has a personal relationship with, which is the norm for an Israeli independent, **L1 is the ceiling for the first version**. L2 might later be acceptable *only* for reminders on an already-sent, owner-approved claim ("תזכורת: זיכוי על חשבונית 4471 עדיין לא התקבל"). That needs Phase-6 credentials and explicit owner opt-in.

### 3.4 Credit-memo chasing

Spendflo's debit-memo agent shows the whole loop as a state machine: drafts the memo for a short delivery → tracks until the supplier issues a matching credit memo or disputes it → escalates unresolved items ([Spendflo debit memo agent](https://www.spendflo.com/agents/ai-debit-memo-processing-agent); [credit memo agent](https://www.spendflo.com/agents/ai-credit-memo-processing-agent)). A May 2026 orchestration write-up describes the same: tolerance check → credit-memo request → only exceptions to a human ([SysGenPro](https://sysgenpro.com/automation/distribution-invoice-automation-to-improve-credit-memo-handling-and-financial-process-accuracy)). Practitioner forums say credits "take weeks and sometimes slip through the cracks" ([EPIUsers](https://www.epiusers.help/t/financial-gurus-process-issues-in-dmr-supplier-credits/34311)). The value lies in *tracking open credits*, not in writing the letter. RestoMatch's recovery reminders already exclude never-claimable types. The EOD digest does not (INT-07 surprise 7). Fix that before adding any chasing automation.

### 3.5 Digest and inbox-zero patterns

Vendors converge on (a) an **exceptions-only queue** (Ramp "Requires review"; Brex "escalates exceptions"), (b) **AI summaries on each card** (Ramp), and (c) **proactive suggested plans** (Xero Cash Flow Actions). I found no vendor publishing digest open rates or "time to inbox zero" metrics (UNVERIFIED gap). For RestoMatch's owner, the digest should lead with the ₪ hero number and include at most 3 T2 act-now items, each carrying one CTA that opens a pre-filled draft. Per INT-07 A6, fold the Ask offline engine's "why the leak changed" fact into the EOD email.

### 3.6 Conversational assistants over financial data: text-to-SQL guardrails

- M1 Finance first let a chatbot query the catalogue directly and got "frequent hallucinations". It switched to having the LLM call the **dbt Semantic Layer**, so queries "conform to vetted metrics and standard query templates" ([dbt × M1 Finance](https://getdbt.com/blog/m1-finance-ai-self-service-claude-dbt)). Vendor case study.
- An arXiv paired benchmark (2604.25149, 2026) finds models apply *stated* business rules reasonably well but "are not, in general, capable of reliably inferring business rules that are not stated anywhere" ([arXiv 2604.25149](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25149)).
- The recommended guardrail stack: schema grounding, semantic definitions, SQL linting, a dry-run `EXPLAIN`, golden-question regression tests, read-only credentials, and human review for finance queries ([Querio](https://querio.ai/articles/llms-hallucinate-sql-failure-modes-how-to-catch); [Wren AI](https://getwren.ai/post/reducing-hallucinations-in-text-to-sql-building-trust-and-accuracy-in-data-access); [Omni](https://omni.co/blog/why-text-to-sql-fails)). Omni notes that repeated LLM-written SQL can give **different numbers for the same question**, which is fatal for a "one number the owner believes" product.
- **RestoMatch status:** Ask uses 4 read-only tools (`leak_hero`, `leak_report`, `leak_breakdown`, `claimable_lines`) and checks every ₪/% in the answer against tool output (INT-07 §2). That is *already* the semantic-layer pattern. **Keep it. Never add a free-form SQL tool.** Add a golden-question regression set (Hebrew phrasing included) to the test suite, owned by test-sentinel.

## 4. Failure modes and incidents of over-automation in money workflows

| Failure | Evidence | Mitigation for RestoMatch |
|---|---|---|
| **Liability for what the bot says** | *Moffatt v. Low-confidence invoices are not matched (INT-03), so no false leak appears |

Track the "flag dismissed as not-a-leak" rate per discrepancy type |
| **Accountability can't be delegated** | "Who signs off when the AI agent pays the invoice?": the loss lands on the finance leader who let it run ([AI Journal](https://aijourn.com/who-signs-off-when-the-ai-agent-pays-the-invoice/)) | Audit trail on every automated decision: which rule, which confidence, which human confirmed |

## 6. Recommendations for RestoMatch (copy / avoid / leapfrog)

**Copy**
1. *Recommend-by-default, scoped opt-in autonomy* (Ramp): per discrepancy type, supplier and ₪ cap, with "requires review" on ambiguity. Proposal for a human PR to `approvals/engine.ts` **[IMM]**.
2. *A risk-class table with permanent human gates* (Microsoft): outbound supplier message, new supplier, bank-detail change, any change to a payable amount. These are never automatic.
3. *A two-number automation disclosure* (Vic.ai): "% invoices nobody edited" and "% nobody opened", shown to the owner alongside the error rate.
4. *Coarse verdicts* (Brex): OK / review / block.
5. *Pre-payment protection* (Xero JAX): unusual amount, changed bank details or new supplier flagged on the pay-run list.
6. *Semantic-layer Ask* (dbt/M1): keep the read-only tools plus the ₪ grounding check, and add a Hebrew golden-question regression set.

**Avoid**
1. Free-form text-to-SQL over money tables.
2. Auto-sending supplier claims (Air Canada liability; owner–supplier relationships; AGENTS.md).
3. "99%" or "up to N hours" marketing claims. Use the customer's own ₪ instead.
4. Building restaurant→supplier ordering as the wedge (Choco and Pepper are well funded on the supplier side).
5. Over-holding: every flag that can't become ₪ (never-claimable types, consolidated invoices) stays T4 neutral.

**Leapfrog**
1. **The restaurant's counter-agent to the distributor's agent**: check the invoice against the order the owner actually sent (WhatsApp text or photo) and against the signed delivery note.
2. **A recovered-₪ metric**, which no AP vendor publishes: "₪ flagged → ₪ claimed → ₪ credited", per supplier.
3. **Supplier-reply classifier**: inbound WhatsApp or email replies to a claim are auto-classified (accepted / credit promised / disputed) and move the claim state. Read-only, Medius-style, no auto-reply.
4. **Ask → action drafts** (INT-07 A5): "draft this week's claims" returns an editable batch with one-tap send.
5. **Continuous leak** (Rillet-style): the hero ₪ updates per matched invoice, not per month.
